LinkedIn Post - SMLP Optimization Tutorial: Optimization meets Formal Methods

🚀 We have just published a hands-on Tutorial https://github.com/SMLP-Systems/smlp/tree/master/tutorial#readme on optimizing systems with focus on formal methods. It includes a real Intel engineering example!

Ever had to tune a complex system: a circuit, a machine, a process?
You can measure or simulate the outputs but don't know the underlying math? 
That's called black-box optimization, and it shows up everywhere: chip design, manufacturing, robotics, drug discovery, and more.

Our tutorial shows how to use SMLP (Symbolic Machine Learning Prover) - an open-source framework that was tested at Intel for optimizing analog hardware designs. Instead of requiring you to write down the math of your system, SMLP learns a model from your measurement data, then searches that model for the best possible parameter configurations and/or inputs - all while respecting whatever constraints you define.
Unlike purely statistical approaches, SMLP leverages Formal Methods to deliver trustworthy results in the presence of noisy or erroneous input data.

The tutorial walks through 4 examples, from textbook to real-world:

🔹 Constrained optimization: find the best solution inside a defined boundary (a unit circle in this case).

🔹 Binh-Korn multi-objective problem: when you have two goals that pull in opposite directions (e.g., minimize cost AND minimize risk), there's no single "best" answer - there's a Pareto front: a curve of optimal trade-offs. SMLP computes it.

🔹 Eggholder function: a classic optimization stress test with hundreds of local minima. A great way to see how SMLP navigates a rugged landscape.

🔹 Intel Signal Integrity I/O channel Optimization example: a production-grade problem with 64 objectives and 4 design parameters, using anonymized Intel data. This is what the tool was actually built for.

For the three first examples results are verified versus analytical solution and SMLP demonstrated great accuracy for all of them.
