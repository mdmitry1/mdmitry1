Ever had to tune a complex system: a circuit, a machine, a process?

You can measure or simulate the outputs but don't know the underlying math? 

That's called black-box optimization, and it shows up everywhere: chip design, manufacturing, robotics, drug discovery, and more.

Tutorial shows how to use 𝐒𝐌𝐋𝐏 (𝐒𝐲𝐦𝐛𝐨𝐥𝐢𝐜 𝐌𝐚𝐜𝐡𝐢𝐧𝐞 𝐋𝐞𝐚𝐫𝐧𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐏𝐫𝐨𝐯𝐞𝐫) an open-source tool proven at Intel for optimizing analog hardware designs. SMLP learns a model from your measurement data, then searches that model for the best possible parameter configurations and/or inputs - all while respecting whatever constraints you define.

Unlike purely statistical approaches, SMLP leverages Formal Methods to deliver trustworthy results in the presence of noisy or erroneous input data.

The tutorial walks through 4 examples, from textbook to real-world:

🔹 Constrained optimization: find the best solution inside a defined boundary.

🔹 Binh-Korn multi-objective problem: when you have two goals that pull in opposite directions and there's no single "best" answer - 𝐒𝐌𝐋𝐏 computes Pareto front: a curve of optimal trade-offs.

🔹 Eggholder function: a classic optimization stress test with hundreds of local minima. A great way to see how SMLP navigates a rugged landscape.

🔹 Intel Signal Integrity I/O channel Optimization example: problem with 64 objectives and 4 design parameters, using mangled Intel data. This is what the tool was actually built for.

For the three first examples results are verified versus analytical solution and SMLP demonstrated great accuracy for all of them.
